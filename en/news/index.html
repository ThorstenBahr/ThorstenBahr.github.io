What is changing

Laws, deadlines, figures – briefly explained and always with a source. So you know what is coming for your wallet and how to position yourself holistically: insurance, provision, tangible assets, energy – and the Driving Licence Compass. Information, not sales.

Driving Licence CompassDriving licence reform05 November 2026Draft / not yet promulgated

Driving licence reform: Bundestag has passed the law – Bundesrat and ordinance still to come

On 5 November 2026 the Bundestag passed the law reforming driver training (amendments to the Driving Instructors Act and the Road Traffic Act) with the votes of CDU/CSU and SPD. Compulsory attendance for theory lessons is dropped, the question catalogue gets smaller, and there is no longer a fixed number of special drives (previously twelve). The practical test is to be shortened from 55 to 40 minutes. The law and the accompanying ordinance still need the Bundesrat’s approval; the start is essentially planned for 1 July 2027. Whether and by how much the driving licence gets cheaper is open: the transport minister speaks of several hundred euros, at best up to €1,000, and the ADAC of up to a third at best – this is not a price promise, and driving schools set their own prices.

To the Driving Licence Compass Source: German Bundestag (text archive, Drs. 21/8979), cross-check: dpa, inside-digital

Additional contribution 2027: ministry sets the average at 2.9%

On 30 October 2026 the Federal Ministry of Health set the average additional contribution rate for 2027 at 2.9% – unchanged from 2026, as the Estimates Panel had recommended. This is a calculated value: each health fund sets its own rate, and on average that is actually around 3.1% (as of October 2026). If your fund raises its rate, you have a special right of termination until the end of the month in which the higher contribution first applies. When comparing funds, we look at the additional contribution and benefits together.

All changes at a glance Source: Federal Ministry of Health (press release 30 Oct 2026), cross-check: vdek

“If there is a power cut and I have no default supplier, I’m left in the dark” – true?

No. The grid operator – not your electricity supplier – is responsible for the line up to your house. Under § 11 of the Energy Industry Act (EnWG) it must fix faults for all households on the grid, no matter who you buy your electricity from. Its fault number is on your bill. And if a supplier goes bust, you automatically fall into the replacement supply (§ 38 EnWG). The lights never go out just because you switched. We have cleared up the five most common myths about switching electricity supplier, with the law and sources.

Insurance & provisionElectricity & gas · context22 October 2026Information

Sign an electricity contract now? What gets more expensive in 2027 – and what doesn’t

The claim “from 2027 electricity prices only go one way” cannot be backed up like this – the picture is mixed. Definitely more expensive: transmission grid charges rise in 2027 from 2.86 to an expected 3.54 ct/kWh because the federal government cuts its subsidy from €6.5 to €5.5 billion. New contracts already cost 38.9 ct/kWh in early October, 1.7 ct more than at the start of the year. On the other hand: local grid charges stay almost the same on national average (regionally from −6% to +5%), and a cut in electricity tax of up to 2.3 ct/kWh has been announced but not decided. Anyone who signs a contract with a price guarantee now secures the procurement price – but beware: many guarantees exclude grid charges, levies and taxes. That is what we look at in the check.

Insurance & provisionElectricity & gas · context22 October 2026Information

Gas 2027: CO₂ price stays within its corridor, grid charges rise sharply in some regions

Three things change for gas customers in 2027: according to the Bundestag decision of 15 October 2026, the CO₂ price stays in the corridor of €55 to €65 per tonne (Finanztip expects the upper end again). Gas grid charges rise by only around €12 a year on national average (model household, 15,000 kWh), but regionally by up to +€145 – or fall by €78. The gas storage levy has been gone since January 2026 (about €58 relief at 20,000 kWh). Procurement prices have risen again since the start of the year. Bottom line: whether a contract with a price guarantee is worthwhile now depends heavily on your grid area – it cannot be said across the board.

Deadline 30 November: 2027 switching season is under way

For most motor insurance contracts expiring on 31 December, the notice of termination must reach the insurer by 30 November. The GDV has published new type classes (for over 12 million drivers) and regional classes for 2027. After a premium increase there is a special right of termination of one month from receipt.

Insurance & provisionElectricity & gas · fact check22 October 2026Information

“If you switch, you can’t go back to the default supply” – true?

No. The default supplier – the provider with the most household customers in the grid area – must supply every household customer at its general prices under § 36 of the Energy Industry Act. So you can switch and go back at any time. Since 2022, default suppliers also may not classify new customers as more expensive than existing ones. The default supply itself can be terminated at two weeks’ notice – and is usually more expensive than a special contract.

To the fact check: 5 myths about switching electricity supplier Source: § 36 EnWG, Federal Network Agency, consumer advice centre

Producer prices in September: +6.7% – heating oil almost twice as expensive as a year ago

The prices manufacturers charge for their goods were 6.7% higher in September 2026 than a year earlier – the strongest rise since March 2023. Energy is the driver (+14.9%): light heating oil cost 91.8% more, natural gas 15.0% and electricity 8.2%. Excluding energy, the rise was 3.2%. These are prices at producer level; in our experience only part of this reaches consumers, and with a delay.

Oil reserve: Bundesrat releases further stocks – mandatory stockholding temporarily drops to 82 days

On 16 October 2026 the Bundesrat approved an ordinance releasing further stocks from the strategic oil reserve. The stockholding obligation temporarily falls from 90 to 82 days. Diesel and heating oil are to be offered first. This is not a payout to consumers and no promise on prices – whether and how strongly heating oil and fuel prices react is open.

Insurance & provisionRetirement provision16 October 2026Draft / not yet promulgated

Early-start pension: Bundestag has passed the law

On 16 October 2026 the Bundestag passed the early-start pension: for children aged 6 to 18 the state provides €10 a month, provided the parents open a pension account. The start is planned for the 2020 birth cohort, and payout is in principle at 65 at the earliest. The law has not yet been promulgated – the Bundesrat still has to deliberate.

What this means for your retirement provision Source: German Bundestag (text archive), cross-check: DATEV
Insurance & provisionTax08 October 2026Draft / not yet promulgated

Income tax reform 2027: Bundestag has debated the draft in first reading

On 8 October 2026 the Bundestag debated the draft Income Tax Reform Act 2027 in first reading and referred it to committee. Among other things it provides for a higher basic allowance (€12,564 from 2027, €12,900 from 2028) and more child benefit (€267 instead of €259 a month from January 2027). It is to be financed in part by a higher rate for very high incomes. The law has not been passed and changes are still possible; the government aims for it to take effect on 1 January 2027.

What this means for your wallet Source: German Bundestag (text archive, Drs. 21/8235), cross-check: DATEV

The federal cabinet has adopted the social insurance reference figures for 2027. The income ceiling for health insurance rises to €6,375 a month, the compulsory insurance threshold presumably to €84,150 a year. The ordinance has not yet been promulgated in the Federal Law Gazette.

Insurance & provisionElectricity & gas07 October 2026Information

Electricity price 2026: 37 cents on average – new contracts more expensive again

In 2026 households pay on average around 37 cents per kilowatt-hour (BDEW: 37.0 ct, Destatis first half-year: 37.97 ct) – less than the year before, mainly because the federal government subsidises grid charges. For contracts that can be newly signed, however, the price is already back at 38.9 ct in early October. The price consists of about one third taxes, levies and surcharges, one quarter grid charges and the rest procurement and sales.

Insurance & provisionLong-term care insurance30 September 2026Draft / not yet promulgated

Care reform: cabinet adopts draft – surcharge for the childless to rise in 2027

On 30 September 2026 the federal cabinet adopted the draft Care Reorganisation Act. Among other things, the surcharge for childless people is planned to rise by 0.3 points to 0.9% from 1 January 2027, and the income ceiling for care insurance contributions is to be raised. From 2028 a contribution surcharge is to apply to spouses and partners co-insured free of charge. The draft still has to pass the Bundestag and Bundesrat – changes are possible.

Property prices: +0.6% year on year – stable, but very different by region

Residential property cost on average 0.6% more in the second quarter of 2026 than a year earlier (new build +1.5%, existing +0.5%). In the seven largest cities, condominiums fell slightly; in large cities outside the metropolises they rose markedly; in rural areas, single- and two-family homes became somewhat cheaper. Construction prices were 5.0% above the previous year at the same time. Whether a purchase is worthwhile therefore depends more than ever on location, condition and financing – not on the national trend.

Insurance & provisionCO₂ price12 August 2026Draft / not yet promulgated

CO₂ price: €55 to €65 per tonne – what that costs when heating

For 2026 the national emissions trading system has a price corridor of €55 to €65 per tonne of CO₂. According to Finanztip, the Bundestag decided on 15 October 2026 to continue this corridor in 2027 as well – instead of the originally planned free market price; promulgation is still pending. At the upper limit of €65 compared with €55, this amounts to about 0.22 ct per kilowatt-hour of natural gas and about 3.2 ct per litre of heating oil (incl. VAT). From 2028 the European emissions trading system is to determine the price.

Statutory health insurance contribution stabilisation act: dentures and co-payments from 2027

The law has been promulgated in the Federal Law Gazette. From 2027 the fixed subsidy for dentures falls from 60% to 50% (60% or 65% with a bonus booklet), and co-payments rise by 50% to €7.50 to €15 per medication. From 2028 a partner surcharge of 2.5% applies in family insurance.

Retirement provision reform from 1 January 2027 adopted

The Bundestag and Bundesrat have adopted the reform of subsidised retirement provision: basic allowance up to €540, child allowance up to €300 a year, minimum own contribution €120, one-off bonus of €200 for savers under 25. Driving licence almost 43% more expensive than in 2020

According to the Federal Statistical Office, prices for driving school and driving licence rose by 42.7% from 2020 to 2025 – general inflation was 21.9%. According to the ADAC, the range for class B in 2025 runs from around €2,466 to €4,476. Official test fees: €24.99 (theory) and €129.83 (practical) per attempt.

Fail rates 2025: 44% theory, 37% practical

The TÜV Association reports for 2025: 44% fail the theory test, 37% the practical test. Under-18s do better (35% and 24% respectively). Anyone who retakes the theory test fails again 56% of the time – good preparation pays off twice.
